# Journey Map — Burnout Recovery Habit Tracker

**Actor:** Maya / Daniel (composite burnout-recovery user)
**Scenario:** Discovering, adopting, and building a sustainable habit-tracking practice while recovering from burnout
**Timeframe:** First 8 weeks of use
**Goal:** Establish a gentle, flexible habit practice that supports recovery without replicating burnout-inducing pressure

---

## Phase Overview

| Phase | 1. Awareness | 2. First Open | 3. Early Use (Days 1–7) | 4. First Hard Week (Days 8–21) | 5. Rebuilding Consistency (Weeks 4–8) | 6. Long-Term Integration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **User goal** | Find something that won't make things worse | Understand if this is safe to try | Build a low-pressure habit log | Survive a bad week without quitting | Experience non-linear progress as normal | Make the app feel like part of recovery, not a chore |
| **Touchpoints** | Therapist referral, Reddit post, App Store search | Onboarding screens, first setup | Daily check-in, habit log | Missed days, app response, support prompts | Weekly reflection, pattern view | Evolved habit set, memory of progress |

---

## Phase 1: Awareness

**What happens:** User is struggling. They've tried other apps, talked to a therapist, or found the app through a Reddit thread on burnout recovery.

**User actions:**
- Searches "habit tracker for burnout" or "gentle habit app no streaks"
- Reads App Store reviews specifically for language about shame or flexibility
- Asks therapist if tracking could help or hurt

**Touchpoints:** App Store listing, Reddit/online communities, word of mouth, therapist recommendation

**Thoughts:**
- "I've failed at this before. What makes this different?"
- "Please don't be another streak app."

**Feelings:** Cautious hope, skepticism, low energy for trying something new

**Opportunities:**
- App Store copy and screenshots must immediately communicate the anti-perfectionism philosophy
- Surface testimonials or copy that names burnout recovery specifically
- Therapist partner program or mention of trauma-informed design principles

---

## Phase 2: First Open — Onboarding

**What happens:** User downloads and opens the app. They encounter onboarding that sets expectations and collects context.

**User actions:**
- Reads onboarding framing screens
- Answers a few context questions (recovery stage, current energy baseline, what "a good day" means to them)
- Sets up 1–3 habits (nudged to start with one)
- Chooses notification style (or opts out entirely)

**Touchpoints:** Onboarding flow, habit creation screen, notification preferences

**Thoughts:**
- "This feels different — it's not asking me how many habits I want to crush"
- "I can skip that? Good. I'm not ready to answer that yet."

**Feelings:** Tentative optimism, some relief, mild anxiety about commitment

**Pain points:**
- Too many setup questions = abandoned onboarding
- Forcing a "streak start" framing before the user is ready
- Any language implying they should be doing more

**Opportunities:**
- Frame onboarding as "getting to know you," not "configure your system"
- Hard cap: no more than 3 onboarding screens before first use
- Default to 1 habit; gently offer to add more only after Day 3
- "No notification" is a first-class option, not a buried setting

---

## Phase 3: Early Use — Days 1–7

**What happens:** User checks in daily (mostly). They log a habit or two, maybe add a mood or energy note. The app responds with low-key encouragement.

**User actions:**
- Opens app once daily (morning or evening)
- Taps to log a habit completion or partial completion
- Optionally adds a one-word mood or energy level
- Reviews what they've logged so far

**Touchpoints:** Daily check-in screen, habit log, in-app micro-copy, optional mood/energy input

**Thoughts:**
- "This takes 30 seconds. That's actually doable."
- "I logged a walk I took. The app counted it. That felt good."

**Feelings:** Fragile momentum, small wins feeling genuinely meaningful, slight wariness about when it will fall apart

**Pain points:**
- Any "you're on a 5-day streak!" language raises stakes and sets up for shame
- Logging feeling like homework when energy is low
- No acknowledgment of partial completion (did some, not all)

**Opportunities:**
- Replace streak count with a soft phrase like "You showed up 5 times this week" (non-consecutive framing)
- Allow "partial" as a first-class log state alongside "done" and "skipped"
- Logging should be completable in under 10 seconds on low-energy days
- First-week micro-copy should normalize "this week doesn't have to be perfect"

---

## Phase 4: First Hard Week — Days 8–21

**What happens:** Life intervenes. A difficult few days, low energy, or an anxiety spike causes the user to miss 3+ days in a row. This is the highest-risk abandonment point.

**User actions:**
- Opens app after several days away (or doesn't)
- Sees days they didn't log
- Reads how the app responds to absence
- Decides whether to continue or quit

**Touchpoints:** App absence response (first re-open after gap), missed day display, optional "how are you?" prompt

**Thoughts:**
- "Here we go — I ruined it."
- "Okay, it didn't yell at me. Maybe I can still use this."
- "It's been 5 days. Is there any point continuing?"

**Feelings:** Shame, resignation, potential relief if app handles it well, anxiety about seeing a visual "void" of missed days

**Pain points:**
- Any visual that makes missing days look like failure (empty cells, broken chains, red indicators)
- "You missed 4 days!" summary screens
- The app going silent (no gentle re-entry prompt after several days away)

**Opportunities:**
- Missing days shown as neutral: no color, no red, no dramatic gap visualization
- Re-entry screen after 3+ days away: warm, non-judgmental — "Good to see you. Want to pick up where you left off, or start fresh this week?"
- "Soft reset" option — user can redeclare their intention without losing history
- This phase is the most important to get right; AB test the re-entry experience heavily

---

## Phase 5: Rebuilding Consistency — Weeks 4–8

**What happens:** User has survived a hard week and returned. They've started to trust the app slightly more. They begin to see patterns — on days they slept well, they were more likely to log. They start adjusting habits.

**User actions:**
- Reviews a weekly or bi-weekly reflection summary
- Edits or swaps a habit that isn't working
- Notices a pattern (e.g., better days follow morning movement)
- Maybe adds a second habit they'd been afraid to add

**Touchpoints:** Weekly reflection view, pattern/insight screen, habit edit flow

**Thoughts:**
- "I didn't realize I'd logged 14 things this month."
- "I do better on days I don't check email in the morning. The app actually shows me that."
- "I want to add stretching but I'm scared of over-committing again."

**Feelings:** Cautious pride, curiosity about their own patterns, growing sense of agency

**Pain points:**
- Reflections framed as performance reviews ("You completed 60% of your habits")
- Insights that compare to previous periods without context
- No ability to edit habits retroactively or restructure the system

**Opportunities:**
- Weekly summary framed as "Here's what I noticed about you this week" — narrative, not numerical
- Pattern insights surface gently: "You logged movement on all 4 days you rated your energy as high. Worth noting."
- "Add a habit" prompt appears only after user views a successful week — timed to confidence, not calendar
- Retroactive logging supported for days they forgot to check in (respects variable schedules)

---

## Phase 6: Long-Term Integration — Week 8+

**What happens:** The app has become part of the user's recovery practice rather than a burden layered on top of it. They use it selectively — some days a full check-in, some days a single tap.

**User actions:**
- Uses app at variable times and depths depending on the day
- Has a small, meaningful set of habits that feel genuinely theirs
- Occasionally reviews their history and feels something positive about it
- May share with therapist as part of check-in context

**Touchpoints:** Flexible daily check-in, history/progress view, export for therapy use

**Thoughts:**
- "This is the first app I've used for more than two months."
- "I showed my therapist my energy log. She found it useful."
- "I don't need a streak. I just want to know I'm still trying."

**Feelings:** Quiet confidence, sense of continuity with their recovering self, reduced shame about imperfect weeks

**Opportunities:**
- "Your recovery story so far" — a narrative, scrollable view of months of gentle progress
- Therapist share/export: clean, context-rich PDF or sharing link
- Annual or milestone reflection: "A year ago you were just starting. Here's what you've done since."
- Graduation framing: when users feel ready, offer a mode that's less structured — "maintenance mode"

---

## Key Moments of Truth

| Moment | Risk | Design imperative |
|---|---|---|
| First app store impression | User bounces if it looks like another performance app | Copy and screenshots must explicitly communicate anti-perfectionism |
| Onboarding completion | User abandons if setup feels overwhelming | Max 3 screens, 1 default habit, no commitment language |
| First partial or skipped day | User expects punishment, may preemptively quit | "Partial" and "skipped" are neutral states with no negative framing |
| Re-opening after 3+ days away | Highest abandonment risk in the product | Warm re-entry screen; soft reset option; no shaming empty-state |
| First weekly reflection | User judges self against numerical targets | Reflection must be narrative, pattern-focused, not percentage-based |
| Month 2 check | User decides if this is a lasting tool or another failed attempt | History view should make 8 weeks feel like meaningful progress |

---

## Design Principles Derived from Journey

1. **Meet the user where they are today, not where they were yesterday.** No comparisons to past streaks or peak performance.
2. **Absence is neutral, not failure.** Missing days have no visual consequence beyond an unfilled space.
3. **Low-energy logging must be effortless.** The hardest days are when the app matters most; one tap should be enough.
4. **Flexibility is a feature, not an excuse.** Variable schedules, retroactive logging, and soft resets are core, not edge cases.
5. **Encouragement is earned through honesty, not inflation.** Don't call 3 habits a "massive win." Trust users to feel genuine satisfaction from authentic acknowledgment.
6. **The app is a companion in recovery, not a manager of it.** Tone, copy, and UX should feel like a calm, non-judgmental friend.

# Empathy Map — Burnout Recovery Habit Tracker

**User archetype:** Adults (25–40) actively recovering from burnout who want to rebuild healthy habits without replicating the performance culture that contributed to burnout.

---

## SAYS
*What users say out loud — in interviews, forums, to friends*

- "I know what I should be doing. I just can't make myself do it."
- "I used to be so disciplined. I don't know what happened to me."
- "Apps make me feel worse when I miss a day. Then I quit."
- "I need something flexible — my energy is completely unpredictable."
- "I don't want to gamify my recovery. It's not a game."
- "I want to notice I'm doing better, but not turn it into a metric."
- "Streaks stress me out more than no tracker at all."
- "My therapist says to be gentler with myself. Habit apps don't know how to do that."

---

## THINKS
*What users think but may not say — internal beliefs, fears, assumptions*

- "If I can't keep a simple habit, something is fundamentally wrong with me."
- "Starting a new system is admitting how far I've fallen."
- "If I track this and fail, I'll have proof that I'm broken."
- "Maybe I need to be consistent for a week before the app is even worth using."
- "My good days will trick me into thinking I'm better than I am."
- "Everyone else seems to be recovering faster."
- "This tool is going to turn into another thing I failed at."
- "I want to celebrate small wins but feel ridiculous doing it."

---

## DOES
*Observable behaviors and actions*

- Downloads habit apps enthusiastically, configures them extensively, then abandons within 4–8 weeks
- Sets up multiple habits at once, then collapses the system when one slips
- Checks the app in the morning, often skips it when the day goes sideways
- Reaches for comfort habits (tea, walks, cooking) but doesn't log them as "real" habits
- Journals when overwhelmed but stops when things feel flat or stable
- Compares current capability to pre-burnout self constantly
- Uses different tools for different needs (mood tracking, journaling, habit streaks) without integration
- Talks to therapists or support communities more than they use apps during hard weeks

---

## FEELS
*Emotional state — underlying anxieties, aspirations, and frustrations*

- **Shame** about inconsistency and perceived regression
- **Fear** that recovery isn't "working" or is taking too long
- **Grief** for their former capable, productive self
- **Relief** on good days — fragile and easily disrupted
- **Resentment** toward systems that make them feel monitored or judged
- **Longing** for routine without the pressure routine used to carry
- **Hope** that small habits can add up to something real
- **Exhaustion** — the very state that makes building new habits hardest

---

## PAINS
*Obstacles, frustrations, risks*

- Streak-breaking causes disproportionate distress and abandonment of the entire system
- Energy and capacity vary drastically day-to-day with no predictable pattern
- Conventional apps reward consistency in ways that shame inconsistency
- Recovery is non-linear; apps built on linear progress models feel dishonest
- Notifications feel like nagging — an external pressure that mimics work culture
- Comparing to pre-burnout benchmarks is always demoralizing
- The act of setting up a new system takes energy they don't have

---

## GAINS
*What success looks like — desired outcomes and measures of value*

- Feeling like today counted, even if it was a low-effort day
- Seeing patterns in their energy or mood without judgment baked in
- A record of how far they've come, not how far they need to go
- Flexibility that adjusts to bad days without punishing them
- Low cognitive load — the app should be easier than not using it
- Gentle reminders that feel like a friend, not a manager
- No public accountability, leaderboards, or social comparison
- The ability to define what "a good day" means for *them*, not a default template

# Personas — Burnout Recovery Habit Tracker

---

## Persona 1: Maya, 34 — The Overachieving Professional

**Tagline:** "I used to be unstoppable. Now I can't finish a to-do list."

### Demographics
- Age: 34
- Occupation: Senior Product Manager at a mid-size SaaS company
- Location: Austin, TX
- Living situation: Rents an apartment alone; long-term relationship, partner lives 30 min away

### Background
Maya built her identity around performance. She was promoted twice in three years, led two major product launches, and prided herself on always being available. Eight months ago she hit a wall — she couldn't focus, stopped sleeping well, and called in sick for the first time in five years. Her therapist named it: burnout. She's now on reduced hours and trying to rebuild.

### Goals
- Regain a sense of daily structure without it feeling like a new pressure
- Rebuild self-trust after months of inconsistency
- Track energy and mood alongside habits to understand her patterns
- Feel like progress — even tiny progress — is real and worth something

### Frustrations
- Conventional habit apps feel like performance dashboards; streaks make her feel worse when she breaks them
- Advice like "just do 5 minutes a day" feels patronizing when she can't predict her energy from day to day
- She keeps setting up elaborate systems then abandoning them, compounding her shame
- Corporate wellness apps from her employer feel surveilled and inauthentic

### Behaviors
- Checks her phone first thing in the morning; anxious scroll through email and Slack
- Journals inconsistently — writes when overwhelmed, skips when flat
- Has tried Habitica, Streaks, and Notion templates; abandoned all three within 6 weeks
- Finds calm in cooking and walking but rarely "counts" them as recovery habits

### Tech Comfort
High. Uses many apps, quick to adopt new tools, but also quick to abandon them.

### Quote
> "I don't need another app that turns self-care into a job."

## Persona 2: Daniel, 27 — The Burned-Out Creative

**Tagline:** "I freelanced my passion into exhaustion and now I don't know who I am without work."

### Demographics
- Age: 27
- Occupation: Freelance graphic designer and illustrator (self-employed)
- Location: Portland, OR
- Living situation: Shares a house with two roommates

### Background
Daniel loved design so much he went freelance at 23. For three years it worked — clients, income, creative satisfaction. Then the hustle caught up with him. He started taking on too much to cover unpredictable income, lost the joy in his work, and developed anxiety around opening his laptop. He's in recovery now: fewer clients, therapy every two weeks, and trying to stop tying his worth to productivity.

### Goals
- Build habits that feel nourishing, not obligatory (drawing for fun, movement, rest)
- Create separation between "work time" and "rest time" — especially hard when home = studio
- Track without comparing himself to past performance
- Find gentle accountability that doesn't feel like external judgment

### Frustrations
- Gamification in habit apps triggers his achievement anxiety — leaderboards and badges are triggering, not motivating
- Variable day structure as a freelancer means rigid daily schedules collapse within a week
- He wants to track recovery habits but not turn recovery into another deliverable
- Most apps assume a 9-to-5 rhythm that doesn't fit his life

### Behaviors
- Sleeps irregularly; some days start at 7am, others at 11am
- Uses physical sketchbooks to avoid screen time but wants digital check-ins to be low-friction
- Has tried Done, Streaks, and a physical bullet journal; inconsistent with all
- Finds comfort in making tea, walking to the park, and short morning stretches

### Tech Comfort
Moderate-high. Prefers visually refined, calm UI. Avoids notification-heavy apps.

### Quote
> "I need something that meets me where I am each day, not where I was yesterday."
